# AQD V1 — optional native WeatherKit research

Scope: user-approved move from V2 to V1; native Swift only. Research checked October 7, 2026 against Apple primary documentation. This document supports the [shared weather design](../design/weather-context.md); no application or generated-image work was performed. Recommendations below are **AQD policy**, not Apple guarantees. This is a product/implementation handoff, not device-tested evidence.

## Verified facts — five primary source groups

1. **[Apple WeatherKit overview](https://developer.apple.com/weatherkit/).** Native Swift API requires iOS 16+; access requires Apple Developer Program membership. Included allowance is **500,000 API calls/month per membership**, not per phone or AQD user. Apple advertises current conditions and 10-day hourly forecasts; minute precipitation and severe alerts are regional. Apple says location is used only for weather forecasts, is not associated with personally identifiable information, and is never tracked between requests. This is Apple's WeatherKit statement, not a blanket privacy statement about Maps/geocoding or AQD.
2. **[Apple WWDC22: Meet WeatherKit](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10003/).** The native demonstration constructs a `CLLocation(latitude:longitude:)`, then directly calls `WeatherService.weather(for:)`. Setup requires enabling WeatherKit for the App ID in the Developer Portal (Capability and App Services) and adding the WeatherKit capability in Xcode. Apple separately describes server-side JWT signing for **REST**. Therefore the native approach does not require an AQD weather backend, embedded REST signing key, or end-user AQD account. That is an architectural conclusion from the native demonstration, not a promise that an unsigned/unentitled app works. Daily forecast is 10 days; hourly is up to 240 hours.
3. **Apple native WeatherKit SDK reference (exact members below).** These are the authoritative contracts for attribution, returned availability, timestamps, and interval bounds:
   - [WeatherAttribution](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherattribution): attribution is required for published software. Obtain it through [`WeatherService.attribution`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherservice/attribution). Use `combinedMarkDarkURL` / `combinedMarkLightURL` appropriate to appearance and `legalPageURL`; do not substitute an invented text-only logo or hardcoded third-party legal destination. `legalAttributionText` exists for apps unable to display the legal page. The overview requires a clearly displayed Apple Weather mark **and** legal link whenever AQD displays Apple weather values. Cache the supplied assets for cached-weather display. Alerts would introduce additional attribution obligations; omit them in minimal V1.
   - [WeatherAvailability](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatheravailability) describes **minute/alert** availability: those can be unsupported or temporarily unavailable. Other datasets such as current weather are expected worldwide and are not represented in this structure. It is **not** a guarantee that a particular future trip date has forecast data.
   - [`WeatherQuery.hourly(startDate:endDate:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherquery/hourly(startdate:enddate:)) permits forecasts up to 10 days (~240 hours) ahead, at most 10 days returned per request, lower bound inclusive / upper exclusive. [`daily(startDate:endDate:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherquery/daily(startdate:enddate:)) likewise limits future forecasts to 10 days; a day is included when its **local midnight** falls in the requested inclusive/exclusive interval. Never promise weather for every future planned date or substitute current conditions for an out-of-range date.
   - [WeatherMetadata](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weathermetadata): `date` is the weather-data request time, `expirationDate` is when data expires, and `location` is the requested location. [`CurrentWeather.date`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/currentweather/date) dates current conditions; [`HourWeather.date`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/hourweather/date) and [`DayWeather.date`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/dayweather/date) identify the start of their forecast period. These are distinct from AQD's local fetch-completion timestamp. Current weather may be modeled, not a literal observation.
4. **[Core Location authorization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/requesting-authorization-to-use-location-services).** Device location is permission-protected; Apple recommends asking only when the user engages a feature needing it and preferring When In Use. AQD policy: prompt only after **Use current location**, provide `NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription`, perform a one-shot foreground lookup, and never request Always/background tracking. Denial must not block manual city selection or the core app.
5. **[CLGeocoder](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clgeocoder).** Forward geocoding converts place/address text into latitude/longitude. It uses a network-based service; detailed placemark results require network access. Apple recommends reusing results and at most one geocoding request per user action; no exact universally safe throughput quota is published here. Thus manually selected cities can supply coordinates without reading device location or prompting for device-location authorization. They do **not** mean coordinate-free weather. Apple's SDK now points developers toward MapKit; treat Apple Maps city search/geocoding as an online dependency, not a guaranteed offline city directory. Persist a previously selected city's resolved coordinates. [`CLPlacemark.timeZone`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clplacemark/timezone) supplies the place timezone when available; handle missing timezone explicitly.

SDK pages were read through Apple's official DocC JSON endpoints where the HTML was JavaScript-rendered; linked public pages remain the canonical citations.

## Recommended minimum V1 contract — AQD policy assumptions

- **Optional and independent:** off until chosen; no AQD login and no Foundation Models availability gate. WeatherKit availability, entitlement, and network are independent of on-device model eligibility. Wardrobe/photos/body measurements never enter weather or geocoding requests. Apple's weather service receives the requested location, even for a manually chosen city; explain this before activation. City text goes to the chosen Apple search/geocoding service, separately from WeatherKit.
- **Small payload:** selected city label, resolved coordinates, timezone; current temperature/condition, daily low/high and precipitation chance for the selected outfit day. Only request daily/current datasets needed by the active view; hourly only if the design actually uses it. No minute feeds, alerts, historical comparisons, background refresh, or movement tracking. Store canonical measurements; format localized °C/°F and other units locally without refetching.
- **Network budget:** one coalesced weather fetch per location + dataset/date-range key; reuse across screens. Foreground-only automatic refresh no more often than every **30 minutes**, and only if expired/missing and the feature is visible. Explicit Refresh can bypass that interval, with one in-flight request and a **60-second cooldown**; no retry loop. Manual city search should debounce (~400 ms), cancel superseded queries, and resolve only the selected result. These are AQD throttles, **not Apple per-device quotas**; 500k is a shared membership allowance, so aggregate usage needs validation before scaling.
- **Small cache:** retain at most five selected-location snapshots locally, up to ten returned daily entries each; no indefinite location history. Freshness ends at the earliest required dataset `expirationDate` or AQD's **30-minute** freshness cap. Expired data may appear only as visibly **“Saved weather — updated [time]”**, at most **24 hours** after AQD fetch time, for the same location/date; never label it current or silently use it as fresh recommendation input. After 24 hours hide numerical weather and say unavailable. Purge weather snapshots when weather is disabled. These retention/stale-display limits are AQD choices, not Apple's documented TTL or a verified license limit.
- **Honest dates:** render forecast periods in the selected location's timezone, including DST; distinguish “Now”, forecast day, and “Updated” time. Store provider timestamps and AQD fetched-at separately. If timezone is unresolved, resolve it or explicitly show timezone unknown rather than silently using the phone's zone. Never fabricate a forecast from a nearby day.

## Native acceptance checklist (for implementation, not yet verified)

- [ ] Signed device build with enabled Developer Program/App ID/capability succeeds without AQD login/backend; missing entitlement/service failure yields a recoverable unavailable state, not a forced sign-in.
- [ ] Enabling weather or picking a manual city does not prompt for device location. Use current location alone requests When In Use; denial/restricted/location-services-off/timeout offers manual city selection and preserves core AQD use. Approximate location is accepted without escalating to precise permission.
- [ ] New city search offline reports that it needs internet; previously resolved city remains selectable. Offline weather has either clearly labeled bounded saved data or an unavailable state with retry; no fabricated weather.
- [ ] Forecast outside returned coverage, empty/missing dataset, or unmatched selected date shows “Forecast not available for this date”; no “no rain” inference from missing data and no current-as-future fallback.
- [ ] Switching city/date rapidly cancels or ignores old responses using a selection/request identity. Late city A cannot overwrite city B; failed new fetch cannot expose city A's cached values as city B.
- [ ] Multiple visible consumers coalesce matching requests; refresh taps do not multiply calls; retries are user-driven and bounded. Expiration alone does not trigger background traffic.
- [ ] Unit changes require no network call; timezone/DST/date-boundary tests use destination local day, not device day. Future forecast date, provider request time, and local fetched-at remain distinguishable.
- [ ] Data expiry is checked on foreground/resume; fresh → saved/expired → unavailable transitions honor the earliest dataset expiry, 30-minute cap, and 24-hour retention rule. Disable clears cached weather.
- [ ] Apple-supplied light/dark mark and legal link appear alongside both fresh and saved weather. Attribution failure must not produce an unattributed numerical-weather card; retry or withhold the card.

Paper handoff: Home uses the owner's compact date-line pattern, short weekday/day + condition symbol + temperature, not a separate weather card. A weather-details task carries selected city/day, forecast low/high/precipitation when available and explicit freshness. Keep a compact Apple-supplied mark + legal link in the weather presentation. Include disabled, city lookup, permission denial, loading, fresh, saved/offline, no-forecast, and retry states.

### Inline-display attribution clarification

[Apple's exact overview rule](https://developer.apple.com/weatherkit/#attribution-requirements): “If your apps, web apps, or websites display any weather data from Apple (other than weather alerts or value-added services or products, as described below), you must clearly display the Apple Weather trademark (Weather), as well as the legal link to other data sources.” The reviewed sources do not explicitly mandate adjacency/on every view, nor authorize attribution hidden only behind a tap. The initial conservative design placed the mark/legal line near Home's date and in details; the owner subsequently removed the Home row. Details retain the references, but details-only compliance is not verified. Resolve this before shipping numerical Home weather; neither placement is a quoted Apple adjacency requirement. No publicly published combined-mark image URL was verified in the checked pages. The Weather app icon is not the wordmark; obtain actual assets from WeatherService.attribution. A clearly annotated Paper slot is placement evidence only, not certified attribution. Preserve planner/generation functionality when weather is unavailable.
